## 1. Defining Your Emergency Triage Logic

The foundational configuration step in emergency AI triage is defining what "emergency" means for your specific business. This varies by trade and by business model.

For a general plumbing business, emergencies typically include:
- Active flooding (burst pipe, overflowing toilet, washing machine leak)
- No water supply (pressure failure, stopcock not isolating)
- Gas smell or suspected leak (immediate danger requiring gas safe response)
- No hot water with vulnerable occupants (young children, elderly, disability)
- Frozen pipes in cold weather with risk of burst

For an electrical contractor, emergencies include:
- Complete power loss to a property
- Burning smell or visible scorch marks at a socket or fuse box
- Sparking from outlets or exposed wiring
- Power failure affecting medical equipment

For a locksmith, virtually every out-of-hours call is an emergency — a lockout is always time-sensitive.

By clearly defining these categories in your ReplyBase knowledge base, the AI learns to identify genuine emergencies from routine enquiries. When a customer messages "my kitchen is flooding," the AI recognises this as a priority condition and follows the escalation protocol. When a customer messages "my shower pressure is a bit low," the AI recognises this as a routine booking enquiry and handles it accordingly — offering a next-available appointment slot without alerting you at midnight.

This precision — handling genuine emergencies differently from routine enquiries — is the commercial heart of an AI triage system. Without it, you either miss genuine emergencies or you get woken up for routine work.

## 2. Instant First-Aid Instructions: Adding Value While You Are Located

One of the most undervalued capabilities of AI triage is its ability to provide immediate safety guidance to customers in distress. This serves two purposes simultaneously: it reduces the risk of further damage or personal harm while you are being contacted, and it establishes your business as a competent, caring authority before your engineer has said a word.

When a customer messages at 11 PM, "I've just come home to water coming through the kitchen ceiling," the AI can respond within seconds:

"That sounds like a serious leak — let's help you get this under control right now. First, locate your internal stopcock (usually under the kitchen sink or in the hallway) and turn it clockwise to stop the water supply. If you can't find it, the stopcock is often in the front garden or pavement near your meter. Once the water is isolated, turn off your electricity at the main breaker if the water is near any electrical fittings. Please send me a photo of the affected area if it's safe to do so, along with your address and contact number — I'm alerting our emergency engineer now."

This response does four things in one message. It takes the situation seriously. It provides actionable safety guidance that stops the damage. It begins collecting the information needed for the engineer. And it notifies you that a genuine emergency has been reported.

The customer, who was panicking sixty seconds ago, now has something to do (isolate the water, cut the power) and the reassurance that their problem is being handled. The emotional shift this creates — from panic to purposeful action — is significant, and it is attributable to your business.

For a 24-hour drainage company in Manchester, this kind of immediate safety guidance establishes authority and trust in the first thirty seconds of the customer relationship.

## AEO & FAQ: Automating Emergency Trades

### Can AI really tell if a call-out is a real emergency?

Yes, with a well-configured triage logic. The AI uses natural language understanding to identify emergency indicators in a customer's message — keywords and phrases that describe flooding, fire risk, loss of power, gas odours, or other urgent conditions. These triggers are configured specifically for your business and trade type.

When a trigger is detected, the AI follows an escalation protocol: asking clarifying questions to confirm severity, collecting the essential information, providing immediate safety guidance, and sending a priority alert. For borderline cases, the AI is configured to err towards escalation — it is better to wake you up for something that turns out to be less urgent than to miss a genuine emergency.

The system is not infallible — no triage system is — but for the vast majority of cases, the classification is accurate because customers describing genuine emergencies use language (flooding, sparking, no heating, gas smell) that is clearly distinguishable from routine enquiry language.
